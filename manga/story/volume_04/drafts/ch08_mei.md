## Chapter engine

Naruto takes the conflict's timetable away from Mei and Yagura. The quiet hill scene shows the cost of that posture: he treats attraction, surveillance, and even his own attention as distractions to control. One week later he makes the same instinct public—he calls a free Kyubi as a deliberate, targeted deterrent, not as a rampage—and forces the war to move.

The chapter must make the causality readable without narration: **quiet camp → Naruto's impatience → Mei cannot stop him → controlled summoning → tower destroyed → Yugao identifies the Konoha consequence → three-day wait for retaliation.** The source calls Mei a rebel leader, not Mizukage; Yagura holds the Mizukage's tower during this sequence (`ch11.txt:49,69-71`).

The hill exchange contains mature allusion in the source. Its adaptation function is Naruto's clinical, goal-first mindset and Mei's surprised interest. Stage it as dialogue and reaction only: no sexualized framing, no flashback, no physical escalation, and no explicit imagery.
