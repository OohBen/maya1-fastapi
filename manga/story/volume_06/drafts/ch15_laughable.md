## Source effect

The chapter spends twelve pages showing the reader everything Naruto has won, and then takes the one
thing he never counted as a possession.

Oto is finished. It has gates, children, businesses, a woman running it who calls him *-sama*, and a
palace he designed himself that is twice the size of the Hokage tower. Karin, who had nothing, gets a
home and a job in it. Jiraiya, the last person in Konoha still actively investigating him, attacks
him, is thrown across a road by his own technique, and decides on the spot to stop looking. By page 12
there is no living person in this story who can lay a hand on him.

Then a man in an orange mask steps out of the air beside him and asks five questions.

They are not threats and they cannot be answered with force, and every one of them is a hole in the
only warm story Naruto has ever told himself. Why did the man abandon a dream he spent an age
preparing? Why was he left alive? Why was the dream never forced on you? Why did he only come for you
after I had gone — when he knew about you the day you were born? And if he cared, why six years?
Obito draws the conclusion out loud, calls it laughable, and disappears into his own eye, satisfied.

**And the source shows us nothing.** No answer, no thought, no expression, no comment. Naruto looks at
the sky, walks off the statue, drops onto the water, walks to the waterfall, closes his eyes, and
jumps. The withholding is not an omission in the prose and it must not be repaired here. It is the
last and largest thing the volume takes off him: for eight chapters the reader has been let into his
head whenever it mattered, and on the last page the door is shut from the inside.

## The ending — how the refusal is built

The withholding only reads as deliberate if the reader can feel the shape of the thing being withheld.
Four mechanisms do that work, and all four are already planted before page 16:

1. **A door that closes.** Naruto has four thought balloons on page 13 — the last four he gets. **From
   page 14 panel 1 to the end of the volume he has no thought balloon at all**, and after page 15 he
   has no spoken balloon either. The reader is admitted to his interior immediately before the attack
   and never again. The shutting of that door is the ending.
2. **Framing A.** One exact close-up of his face recurs five times — p16 panels 2, 4 and 7, p17 panel
   2, p18 panel 1 — identical framing, identical light, identical expression, changing only in what
   stands behind him. By the fourth repetition the reader is actively searching it for a change. The
   fifth is the reaction shot, and it contains nothing.
3. **Somebody else already failed.** Chapter 13 page 6 ends on Zetsu thinking "NOTHING ON HIS FACE. AS
   ALWAYS", and this chapter's page 12 ends on Jiraiya giving up on reading him entirely. Two
   characters try and fail before the reader is asked to.
4. **The world does not react either.** One environmental SFX — the waterfall — is established on page
   13 and is the **only** SFX in the whole Valley sequence. It is unchanged on the last page. Nothing
   in the frame acknowledges that anything happened.

Page 18 therefore carries **zero balloons, zero thoughts and zero captions** and ends on the empty lip
of the falls. The jump itself is elided between panels 7 and 8. That elision is the last withholding
and it is intentional.

## Final dialogue and executable `name`

Reading order is right-to-left within each page. Coordinates use page width `x=0–100` from left to
right and page height `y=0–100` from top to bottom. Panel 1 is always the top-right panel; within a
row, a lower-numbered panel is always further right. Balloon numbers restart on every page and are
final lettering order; within a panel the first-read balloon sits further right or higher. Every
balloon record gives placement and a visible tail target or explicitly states `OFF`/tail-less.

Integrated ImageGen lettering is intended. During production, each generated page must be compared at
full size with the exact page/panel/speaker order below; any missing, duplicated, misspelled, moved,
or misattributed line fails the page and requires editing or regeneration.

**Standing visual locks for this chapter.** The **Eternal Mangekyō is open in both of Naruto's eyes in
every panel he appears in**; the right-side bang covers the right eye and the visible eye is his left,
except in the two panels named in the Visual QA. He wears **full armour, carries the Gunbai on his
back, and is bare-headed — no forehead protector anywhere in this chapter.** Every written surface —
the paper handed to the guard, Guren's desk, the library scroll, any shop sign in Oto — is ILLEGIBLE
SCRIBBLE, never readable words. The only deliberate lettering is the chapter marker and the four
cards.

**Framing A.** One exact close-up recurs five times and must be drawn **identically** each time:
Naruto's head and shoulders in three-quarter view from his left side, **facing frame left**, the same
size in the panel, the same light, the same completely neutral expression, the visible left eye
carrying the Eternal Mangekyō. It appears on **p16 panels 2, 4 and 7; p17 panel 2; and p18 panel 1.**
The only thing permitted to differ between the five is what stands behind him. If any of the five is
redrawn at a different scale or with a different expression, the ending fails.

**Scene geometry locks — four scenes, four fixed axes, and they never invert.**

1. **Oto, gate and street (pp. 1–4):** Naruto and Karin come from **frame right** and move **leftward**
   into the village throughout; the guard and the gates are **frame left** on approach. Once inside,
   **Naruto is ahead at frame left and Karin follows at frame right**, per ch21:375.
2. **The palace office (pp. 5–8):** **Guren is at the desk at frame left and faces right; Naruto is
   seated at frame right and faces left; Karin sits between them, to Naruto's left.** This is
   deliberately the identical blocking to the Hokage's office in Chapters 5 and 13, and the rhyme is
   the point — same room shape, same chair, except the woman behind this desk works for him.
3. **The road outside Oto (pp. 9–12):** **Naruto is frame left and moving leftward; Jiraiya arrives
   behind him at frame right.** The Ōdama Rasengan therefore travels **right to left, with the reading
   flow**, and the reflection sends it **left to right, against it.** That reversal of direction is how
   the page states that the technique came back.
4. **The Valley of the End (pp. 13–18):** **Naruto is frame left throughout and Obito is frame right
   from his arrival to his departure.** Hashirama's statue stands across the gorge at **frame right**;
   the waterfall and the deeper country are at **frame left and below**. Naruto faces right only when
   he is looking at Hashirama or at Obito, and **faces left for every step he takes after page 15.**
