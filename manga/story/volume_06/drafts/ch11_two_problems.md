## Source effect

The volume's title is stated here, out loud, by the man it is about. He sits in the seat of the man
who raised him, in a room whose centrepiece has been stolen, and delivers a medical report on his own
extinction in the tone of a supply requisition. Nothing about him is frightened. That is the horror:
Zetsu — a thing that eats people — is the one who cannot keep still.

The chapter has to do two jobs the prose does in a paragraph each. First it must give the reader the
**geometry of the threat**, because from here on every Amaterasu, every Mokuton, every Susano'o in
this series costs him something, and the reader has to be able to feel that price in a later panel
without being told again. Second it must make the contingency land as what it is: he is not making a
brave speech about dying. He is scheduling it, assigning it to a subordinate, and specifying that the
man who will resurrect him must be kept in working order — because he wants to fight him afterward.

Then the chapter turns outward and demonstrates, in three quick transactions, exactly how much of the
world he is now moving without touching it: Konoha's medics poached with a stolen face, the
Tsuchikage allowed to dance because a murder has priority, and a village left to be attacked so he
can find out how strong it is. He sends the counters he withheld from Jiraiya — not mercy, an
experiment with a control. And the last thing he says is that he wants it filmed.

## Final dialogue and executable `name`

Reading order is right-to-left within each page. Coordinates use page width `x=0–100` from left to
right and page height `y=0–100` from top to bottom. Panel 1 is always the top-right panel; within a
row, a lower-numbered panel is always further right. Balloon numbers restart on every page and are
final lettering order; within a panel the first-read balloon sits further right or higher. Every
balloon record gives placement and a visible tail target or explicitly states `OFF`/tail-less.

Integrated ImageGen lettering is intended. During production, each generated page must be compared at
full size with the exact page/panel/speaker order below; any missing, duplicated, misspelled, moved,
or misattributed line fails the page and requires editing or regeneration.

**Global geometry lock — the whole chapter is one axis and it never rotates.** The hall runs left to
right. **The throne is at the frame-right end and Naruto is on it; the empty Gedō Mazō socket is at
the frame-left end and Zetsu stands between them, always frame-left of Naruto, in every panel of the
chapter without exception.** Zetsu may walk a slow half-circle but never crosses behind or to the
right of the throne. Every balloon of Naruto's that must exit a panel exits the **right** edge; every
balloon of Zetsu's that must exit exits the **left**. The single moment the axis is under pressure is
page 14 panel 7, where Naruto leaves — and it is solved in depth rather than by swapping sides: he
stays in the **near foreground at frame right**, back to camera, walking away into the hall, while
Zetsu stands in the middle ground at frame left. He never appears to Zetsu's left.

**Standing visual locks for this chapter.** Naruto is in plain black with **no forehead protector, no
armour, no Gunbai, no sword and no pouch** on every page. His irises carry the **ordinary three-tomoe
Sharingan from page 1 to page 13 panel 5** and the **Eternal Mangekyō from page 13 panel 6 to page
14 panel 7** — the transition happens on page and is the chapter's only visual event. The heavy right
bang covers his right eye except in page 13 panel 6, which is the one panel in the chapter framed on
**both** eyes so the reader can see the pattern change in both of them. **No medicine bottle appears.
No jutsu is performed. Nothing is destroyed, cut, burned or grown.** The only written surfaces in the
room are the old wall tablets, which are ILLEGIBLE SCRIBBLE and are never read, quoted, or brought
into focus. The only deliberate lettering is the chapter marker and the one card.

**Border locks.** Present-tense panels have ordinary borders. Page 3 panels 2 and 3 (the chakra
diagram) and page 5 panels 2, 4 and 6 (the three cost plates) carry a **soft-edged border** and are
the only non-present panels in the chapter. The three cost plates are recollections of Chapter 4 and
must be recognisably the same images the reader has already seen, not new compositions.
